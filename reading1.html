Tristan Burchard
Reading #1

In my eyes, photography is a spectrum of expression that varies from picture to picture. At times, I find myself taking photos in a particular manner to capture a view from the perspective I find pleasing, while at other times it's simply a means of solidifying a moment that I’d like to look back on. On the other hand, sometimes I find myself capturing something that I believe tells a story or even something as simple as a feeling. Each and every moment that I feel compelled to photograph usually comes from a varied intent that’s based on the context of the moment and the feeling it gave me.

Sontag makes a point to highlight photography's subtlety that can go completely undetected at first glance. She mentions the notion that paintings, drawings, and writings of a person or event are perceived as interpretations rather than “statements of the world”; whereas, on the other hand, photographs are viewed as pieces of the moment, giving them a feeling of accuracy in terms of depicting a moment. I think this is interesting because I agree that photos have this quality to them on the surface, though we often forget (not as much with AI now) that within a picture there is always room to leave in or out. In other words, they are still vulnerable to manipulation even if it isn’t evident in the picture itself, though with further context it might become visible.

Rawness and vulnerability through photography and/or illustration is what we need to represent the notions of our world at the moment. We live in a world of many masks of all kinds, all playing their part to shape their piece into what they think or have been told will make it fit. Comparison and the strive to compete are innate to our being, though the access we are granted to the world and all its opinions through the news, social media, etc puts that nature into overdrive on steroids.
Capturing moments for what they are without any form of manipulation (outside of the consumer's perspective) is necessary in our current world, as I believe we need more reminders of how imperfect we truly are. Pictures and videos of our world’s beauty itself are often heavily boosted in saturation, hues, and all kinds of adjustments to create a prettier picture. I don’t think this is inherently bad and is a form of one's expression; however, for uncuscepting individuals, these manipulations can paint one side of the world in a brighter or even happier light than the one that individual lives in. Though geographic phenomena such as the Grand Tetons are still magical with or without these slight adjustments, they often still push a subconscious draw towards the image/video, which can inadvertently leave one blind to the beauty of the place they lie within. I think it’s important to let people see the raw and be left with a baseline to build off of instead of being given expectations higher than reality can hold itself up to.
