Reading #2

I think this study is one of the more predictable studies in terms of outcome, as the results were spot on with what I’d expect from a comparison in brain activity when receiving external help versus not. However, the combination of stark and subtle implications that derive from these results is what brings about concern.

In the study, it was shown that the LLM-Brain group had 47% less brain activity than the Brain-LLM group. This short study only covered a small, non-diverse crowd of people selected from a specific geographical location, so it’s hard to say that the results would apply so intensely to the average person. However, it still speaks to the lack of effort that comes with using LLMs as a solution rather than an assist. It begs the question: what do unsuspecting youth have in the way of their cognitive growth and awareness when LLMs are so easily accessible?

When I look back on my upbringing and the opinions I had about the technology we had, I was very rebellious to the notion that technology was harmful; as one does as a young child because we all know children love to do and feel the opposite of what they’re told. With that said, growing up and swimming through the motions of life, I now see and feel the consequences that quick solutions/dopamine had on me; my attention span is six feet under, and I’m often antsy, searching for the next form of entertainment instead of living in the present with myself, bored.

I can’t say everyone will be the same, though I’d imagine a lot of our newer generations will go through these same developmental stages in a somewhat adjacent fashion. Though this time, it’ll be through a world with truly unlimited convenience. Humans grow mostly through our struggles, rather than the times when things were easy. In a world where access to the easy way out of every step of education is available in a couple of taps, there’s no saying it will be resisted. Kids will be kids and make their mistakes; this time, the consequences, I would imagine, will be far more detrimental, with higher chances of cognitive decline, dementia, and overall lack of education.

Aside from the physiological factors that come into play with a lack of using your brain, the societal effects that come with lack of proper education. When you don’t know something, it's very easy to fill in the gap with something pretty, though not everything pretty is true. The more illiterate, uneducated, and cognitively slower we get, the easier we are to be controlled through censorship, propaganda, and all forms of manipulation. The further down the line of cognitive decline that we go, the more susceptible we will become to sweet lies that fill the gap. The future we’re building feels like a pyramid, one where the aware sit at the top and the unaware lie at the bottom; and with each generation, the bottom of the pyramid gets lower and lower.
